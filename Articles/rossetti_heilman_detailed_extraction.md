## Paper 1: Rossetti et al. 1998

**Title:** *Prism adaptation to a rightward optical deviation rehabilitates left hemispatial neglect*

### 1. Provide a detailed, step-by-step description of the experimental procedures

#### 1.1 Overall study structure

- The paper reports **two experiments**.
- The total patient sample across the paper is **16 right brain-damaged patients with persistent left hemispatial neglect**.
- The paper states that patients participating in both experiments were enrolled with a **minimal time interval of one week** and in a **random order**.

#### 1.2 Experiment 1: step-by-step procedure

- Purpose stated in the paper: measure the **adaptability of neglect patients to a lateral shift of the visual field**.
- Participants: **8 neglect patients** and **5 control subjects**.

Step-by-step procedure reported in the paper:

1. Subjects were seated in front of a **horizontal box** that permitted measurement of finger movement endpoints with an accuracy of **1 degree**.
2. For the straight-ahead tests, subjects were **blindfolded**.
3. In the pre-test, blindfolded subjects were required to **point straight ahead** while their head was kept aligned with the body’s sagittal axis.
4. The pre-test contained **10 straight-ahead pointing trials** and was performed **without goggles**.
5. Subjects then underwent prism exposure.
6. The methods state that all subjects in experiment 1 were randomly subjected to **two different wedge prism exposure conditions**:
   - **base left**
   - **base right**
7. The order of these two exposure conditions was **counterbalanced**.
8. The minimum time interval between these two exposure conditions was **two days**.
9. During prism exposure, subjects made **50 pointing responses** to visual targets presented **10 degrees to the right or left of the objective body midline**.
10. During prism exposure, subjects were asked to point at a **fast but comfortable speed**.
11. During prism exposure, subjects could see:
   - the **target**
   - the **second half of their pointing trajectory**
   - their **terminal error**
12. During prism exposure, the head was kept aligned with the body’s sagittal axis by a **chin-rest** and controlled by an investigator.
13. The duration of the exposure period ranged from **2 to 5 minutes**.
14. Immediately upon removal of the **10-degree prism**, a post-test was run.
15. The post-test again consisted of **10 straight-ahead pointing trials**.

Details not reported in the paper:

- The exact order of the left and right exposure targets within the 50 pointing responses is **not stated**.
- Whether those exposure targets were randomized, alternated, or blocked is **not stated**.
- Any explicit familiarization phase before the pre-test is **not stated**.
- Any explicit reset procedure between individual pointing responses is **not stated**.

#### 1.3 Experiment 2: step-by-step procedure

- Purpose stated in the paper: investigate whether prism adaptation could improve the **main clinical manifestations of neglect**.
- Participants: **12 neglect patients**.
- Group allocation: patients were **randomly assigned** to:
  - a **prism group**
  - a **control group**

Step-by-step procedure reported in the paper:

1. All patients underwent a **similar procedure**.
2. A first testing session (**pre-test**) was performed.
3. This pre-test session consisted of a **series of traditional neuropsychological tests**.
4. After the pre-test session, patients performed the **elementary pointing task** used in experiment 1.
5. This exposure task was used to adapt subjects to the **visual-proprioceptive discrepancy**.
6. Patients in the **prism group** were exposed to a **10-degree optical deviation to the right**.
7. Patients in the **control group** wore **neutral goggles with thick flat glasses**.
8. Immediately after removing the goggles, a second testing session (**post-test**) was performed.
9. The post-test used the **same battery of tests** as the pre-test.
10. Patients were tested again after a delay of **about two hours**.
11. This third session is described in the paper as the **late test**.

Because the paper states that the prism exposure condition was **identical in the two experiments**, the exposure phase in experiment 2 consisted of:

1. **50 pointing responses**
2. to visual targets presented **10 degrees to the right or left of the objective body midline**
3. at a **fast but comfortable speed**
4. with visibility of the **target**, the **second half of the pointing trajectory**, and the **terminal error**
5. with head alignment maintained by **chin-rest**
6. over an exposure period of **2 to 5 minutes**

Details not reported in the paper:

- Trial counts for each separate neuropsychological test are **not stated**.
- Timing for each separate neuropsychological test is **not stated**.
- Any block structure within the clinical test battery is **not stated**.
- Any explicit familiarization phase before the clinical pre-test is **not stated**.

### 2. Describe precisely how the visuomotor shift was introduced

- The paper contrasts this with a previous study that shifted **only the left half of the visual field**.
- In this paper, the shift is produced by physical prism goggles rather than by shifting a cursor, controller, or virtual hand.
- During exposure, subjects could see the target and part of their own real pointing movement, so the reported perturbation affected the seen relation between the visual scene/target and the participant’s own hand.

### 1. Provide a detailed, step-by-step description of the experimental procedures

#### 1.1 Overall study structure

- This paper is a **review article**, not a single original prism-adaptation experiment.
- The abstract states that the article discusses:
  - how to **assess** patients for neglect
  - the **pathophysiology** of neglect
  - the **treatment** of neglect

#### 1.2 Step-by-step procedures explicitly described in the paper

Because this is a review, there is **no single experimental procedure** with baseline, prism exposure, and post-exposure assessment. The paper instead describes several bedside assessment procedures.

Step-by-step procedures explicitly described in the paper include:

1. **Testing for inattention**
   - Present stimuli or no stimuli to the **ipsilesional** or **contralesional** side in **random order**.
   - Each time a stimulus or nonstimulus is presented, the examiner says **“now.”**
   - If the patient makes more contralesional than ipsilesional errors, this is evidence of **inattention**.
2. **Testing extinction**
   - The paper states that some patients recover enough to detect isolated stimuli, but when presented with **two or more simultaneous stimuli**, they may fail to detect the contralesional one.
3. **Line bisection**
   - Present a line and ask the patient to find the **middle of the line**.
4. **Cancellation**
   - Place a sheet with targets before the patient and ask the patient to **mark out or cancel all the targets**.
   - The paper states that the examiner should observe the direction in which the patient explores the sheet.
5. **Drawing**
   - Test by having the patient **copy** the examiner’s drawing and by having the patient **draw spontaneously**.
6. **Crossed-response task**
   - The paper states that attentional and intentional neglect can be dissociated with a crossed-response task in which the subject acts in **ipsilateral space when presented contralesional stimuli** and in **contralesional space when presented ipsilesional stimuli**.
7. **Fixed-aperture technique**
   - The paper states that representational and intentional deficits can be dissociated with a fixed-aperture technique.
   - In one half of the trials, the patient moves the **top sheet**.
   - In the other half of the trials, the patient moves the **lower sheet**.
8. **Reference-frame dissociation**
   - The paper states that body-centered versus environmentally centered neglect can be dissociated by having patients bisect lines **upright** and then while lying on their **right** or **left** side.
9. **Personal neglect testing**
   - Ask the patient to **show or point to** different parts of the right or left side of the body.
   - The paper also describes a cancellation-type task using **sticky paper** on different body parts.
   - It also describes a bisection-type task in which a horizontal line is placed before the subject, who is asked to point to the position directly across from the **middle of the chest**.
10. **Representational imagery testing**
   - Ask patients to recall details of a **familiar scene** or to imagine a **contralesional part of the body**.

Details not reported in the paper:

- A single participant sample for one prism experiment: **not stated**
- Number of repetitions or trials for most of these bedside procedures: **not stated**
- Any prism exposure phase: **not stated**
- Any post-exposure after-effect procedure: **not stated**
