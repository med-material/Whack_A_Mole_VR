Our project is about prism adaptation therapy (prism therapy) for unilateral spatial neglect (USN) in VR. Generally speaking, prism therapy involves exposure to wedge prism glasses that induce a lateral optical shift to the ipsilesional side of the brain lesion and a pointing or reaching task to induce prism adaptation. Prism therapy is generally comprised of a pre-exposure phase for assessing baseline performance using some kind of rehabilitation task, then an exposure phase using the pointing or reaching task with the wedge prism glasses which causes a visual mismatch and initial pointing errors that gradually corrects itself through "error reduction", and then a post-exposure phase without the glasses to assess if and how prism adaptation has been induced, usually analysing the "after-effects" and its transfer to higher-order spatial processes. This is just based on some of the earlier papers on prism therapy, specifically Rosetti1998, Frassinetti 2002, and Serino 2005/2006. 

We need a detailed literature review to support and answer the following:
- Researchers in this field fail to use consistent terminology for addressing and defining or interpreting prism adaptation and the prism effect and how.
- There is no shared framework of standardized facilitation of prism therapy in VR, every paper essentially reinvents their own implementation despite some papers having similar goals or could have used the same platform for their research.
- Papers typically only address extreme symptoms of USN, despite more sensitive subtypes existing, which might limit clinical relevance.

For that purpose, we want to go through every single paper and answer the following. We're looking at detailed nuances in interpretation and use of terminology, very specific details on procedure, implementation and application of tasks: like do they use controllers, do they have participants press a button to confirm or implement some other way that does not risk the Heisenberg effect ( The Heisenberg Effect in the context of controllers refers to the phenomenon where a discrete input, such as a button press, disturbs the position of the tracker, leading to different selection points during interaction.), do they reset their position and when, etc., I hope you get the idea.
